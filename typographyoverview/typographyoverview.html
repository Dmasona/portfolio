Typography Overview

Typography is the art and technique of arranging type to make written language legible, readable, and appealing when displayed. This includes choosing a font, adjusting its parameters, and organizing the text on the page.

Key Typography Terms

Ascender Line (n.)
The guideline that marks the uppermost point of an ascender, which is the part of a lowercase letter extending above the x-height.
Cap Height (n.)
The height of an uppercase letter from the baseline to the top of the letter.
Serif (n.)
Small projections or "feet" at the ends of strokes in certain typefaces. Serif fonts enhance readability in long passages of text.
Bracket (n.)
The curved or angled connection between a serif and the main stroke of a letter. The shape and contrast of the bracket contribute to a typeface’s style.
Terminal (n.)
The end of a stroke that does not have a serif. This can be rounded, flat, or angled, depending on the typeface.
Loop (n.)
A fully closed or partially closed counter at the bottom of certain lowercase letters, such as "g."
Stem (n.)
The primary vertical or diagonal stroke of a letterform, providing structural support.
Arm (n.)
A horizontal or upward-slanting stroke that does not connect to another stroke at one end, as seen in letters like "T" and "Y."
Typeface (n.)
A typeface encompasses a family of characters that share a common design theme.
Font (n.)
Distinct from a typeface, a font refers to a specific weight, size, and style within a typeface family.
Baseline (n.)
The invisible line on which most characters sit, providing consistency in alignment.
