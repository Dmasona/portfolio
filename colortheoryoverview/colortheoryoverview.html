Color Theory Overview

Color Theory is the study of how colors interact and affect human perception. Color theory enables you to select colors that work well together to create visually appealing designs that will convey the right message or mood.

Key Color Theory Terms

Color Wheel (n.)
The color wheel is a fundamental tool in color theory, illustrating the relationships between colors.
Primary Colors (n.)
Primary colors are the three basic colors red, blue, and yellow, that cannot be created by mixing other colors.
Secondary Colors (n.)
Secondary colors are created by mixing two primary colors. Green, violet, and orange are the secondary colors.
Tertiary colors (n.)
Tertiary colors result from mixing a primary color with a secondary color.
Saturation (n.)
Saturation refers to the intensity and purity of a color.
Hues (n.)
Hues are often defined as the purest form of a color.
Shade (n.)
a darker value of a color, made by adding black.
Tint (n.)
the lighter value of a color created when a hue is blended with white.
Tone (n.)
a color created when a hue is blended with gray; adding gray quiets or tones down a color.
Value of the Hue (n.)
The value of a hue refers to its relative lightness or darkness.
Neutral Colors (n.)
Neutral colors are often perceived as the absence of color, but they usually contain subtle undertones.
Warm colors (n.)
Warm colors bring energy and intensity to a design. They often evoke strong emotions and can create a sense of excitement or warmth.
Cool Colors (n.)
Cool colors contribute to a calm and soothing atmosphere. These colors are associated with relaxation and can evoke a peaceful, tranquil feeling.
Monochromatic Colors (n.)
A monochromatic color scheme consists of a single hue with variations in shades and tints. This approach creates a minimal yet impactful design, directing attention to key elements.
Color Scheme (n.)
A color scheme is the combination of all the colors used in a project, arranged in a way that enhances the overall design.
Triadic Colors (n.)
Triadic colors are a group of three colors that are evenly spaced on the color wheel.
Complementary Colors (n.)
Complementary colors are pairs of colors that, when combined, cancel each other out by producing a grayscale color like white or black. When placed next to each other, they create the strongest contrast and reinforce each other.
Split Colors (n.)
Split colors, a fundamental concept in color design, involves using variations of color families to create visual interest and harmony. This technique focuses on splitting complementary colors by incorporating analogous hues, which helps generate a more dynamic and visually appealing composition.
Analogous Colors (n.)
Analogous colors refer to groups of colors that are next to each other on the color wheel.
Grayscale (n.)
a full range of values from white to black simplified into a graduated scale.
Color Harmony (n.)
a satisfying balance or unity of colors.
