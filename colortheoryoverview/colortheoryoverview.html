Color Theory Overview

Color Theory is the study of how colors interact and affect human perception. Color theory enables you to select colors that work well together to create visually appealing designs that will convey the right message or mood.

Key Color Theory Terms

Color Wheel (n.)
The color wheel is a visual representation that illustrates the relationships between colors.
Primary Colors (n.)
Primary colors are the three basic colors red, blue, and yellow from which all other colors are derived.
Secondary Colors (n.)
Secondary colors are created by mixing two primary colors together. For example, red and blue make purple, blue and yellow make green, and yellow and red make orange.
Tertiary colors (n.)
Tertiary colors are made by mixing one primary color and an adjacent secondary color. For example, red (primary color) and orange (secondary color) blended together produce the tertiary color red-orange.
Saturation (n.)
Saturation is the intensity and purity of a color or hue.
Hue (n.)
A hue is the purest and brightest form of a color.
Shade (n.)
A darker value of a color, made by adding black.
Tint (n.)
The lighter value of a color created when a hue is blended with white.
Tone (n.)
A color created when a hue is blended with gray.
Value of the Hue (n.)
The value of a hue refers to the lightness or darkness of a color.
Neutral Colors (n.)
Neutral colors are hues that seem to lack color and cannot be found on the color wheel. For example, black, white, and gray.
Warm colors (n.)
Warm colors are colors that convey a sense of warmth to a viewer. For example, red, orange, and yellow.
Cool Colors (n.)
Cool colors are colors that convey a sense of coolness to a viewer. For example, blue, green, and purple.
Monochromatic Color Scheme (n.)
A monochromatic color scheme uses a single hue that consists of different variations of that respective hue's shades and tints.
Color Scheme (n.)
A color scheme also referred to as a color palette is a planned arrangement or group of colors that are going to be used together in a project.
Triadic Colors (n.)
Triadic colors are a combination of three colors that are equally spaced out from one another around the color wheel. For example, red, yellow, and blue or green, purple, and orange.
Complementary Colors (n.)
Complementary colors are the colors that are positioned directly across from one another on the color wheel. For example, red and green, blue and orange, or yellow and purple.
Split-Complementary Color Scheme (n.)
A split-complementary color scheme is a color scheme that uses a base color and the two colors adjacent to its complement. For example, if the base color is blue, the split-complementary colors would be orange and red.
Analogous Colors (n.)
Analogous colors are colors that are directly next to each other on the color wheel. For example, red, orange, and yellow are analogous colors.
Grayscale (n.)
A grayscale is a full range of values from white to black simplified into a graduated scale.
Color Harmony (n.)
A color harmony is a satisfying balance or unity of colors.
